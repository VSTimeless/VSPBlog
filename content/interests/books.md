# Books That Inspire and Inform Me

Reading has always been a cornerstone of my personal and professional development. Books offer a window into different worlds, perspectives, and knowledge domains. Here are some that have made a significant impact on my thinking:

## Currently Reading

- **"The Pragmatic Programmer" by Andrew Hunt and David Thomas**  
  A classic in software development that offers practical advice for improving as a programmer and problem solver.

- **"The Innovators" by Walter Isaacson**  
  An exploration of the people who created the computer and the internet, providing valuable historical context for modern technology.

- **"Never Split the Difference" by Chris Voss**  
  Negotiation tactics from a former FBI hostage negotiator that are surprisingly applicable to everyday professional interactions.

## Technical Favorites

- **"The Art of Electronics" by Paul Horowitz and Winfield Hill**  
  My go-to reference for electronics design. The clear explanations and practical approach make complex concepts accessible.

- **"Code: The Hidden Language of Computer Hardware and Software" by Charles Petzold**  
  A brilliant journey from basic electrical circuits to modern computers, explaining how everything connects.

- **"Designing Digital Systems with SystemVerilog" by Frank Bruno**  
  This book transformed my understanding of hardware description languages and FPGA design.

## Science & Popular Science

- **"Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!" by Richard P. Feynman**  
  Feynman's curiosity and approach to problem-solving continue to inspire my work as an engineer.

- **"Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind" by Yuval Noah Harari**  
  A thought-provoking look at human history that challenges our assumptions about society and progress.

- **"The Design of Everyday Things" by Don Norman**  
  Essential reading on user-centered design principles that I apply to my engineering projects.

## Fiction & Philosophy

- **"Dune" by Frank Herbert**  
  Beyond being a gripping sci-fi epic, it offers fascinating explorations of ecology, politics, and human potential.

- **"Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance" by Robert M. Pirsig**  
  A philosophical journey that examines the relationship between people, technology, and quality.

- **"The Three-Body Problem" by Liu Cixin**  
  Hard science fiction that presents fascinating scientific and philosophical questions in a captivating narrative.

## Reading Habits

I try to read at least 25 books each year, balancing technical material with fiction and philosophy. I've found that this broad reading approach helps me connect ideas across disciplines and think more creatively about engineering challenges.

I generally read physical books for deeper technical study (with plenty of margin notes and highlights) and use an e-reader for fiction and lighter non-fiction, especially when traveling.
