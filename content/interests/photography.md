# Photography: Capturing Technology and Nature

Photography has become a meaningful creative outlet that complements my engineering work. While I'm fascinated by the technical aspects of cameras, what truly draws me to photography is the opportunity to document both the technological world I study and the natural world that inspires so much innovation.

## My Photography Journey

My interest in photography began as an extension of my engineering mindset. Understanding the optics, mechanics, and digital processing involved in modern cameras appealed to my technical curiosity. However, as I practiced more, I discovered that photography also exercises a different part of my brain—encouraging me to focus on aesthetics, composition, and storytelling.

## What I Photograph

My photography generally falls into three categories:

### 1. Electronics and Projects

I document my engineering projects from concept to completion. These photos serve both as a record of my work and as a way to communicate technical concepts visually. I'm particularly interested in macro photography of electronic components, revealing the intricate details normally invisible to the naked eye.

### 2. Architecture and Urban Landscapes

I'm fascinated by the intersection of technology and urban environments. I enjoy photographing modern architecture, infrastructure, and the ways technology shapes our cities. There's something compelling about capturing the geometric patterns and structural elements of the built environment.

### 3. Natural Landscapes

As a counterbalance to technology, I find peace in photographing natural landscapes. Whether it's mountain vistas, flowing water, or intricate natural patterns, nature photography helps me disconnect and find inspiration outside the digital realm.

## Equipment

I shoot with a modest setup that emphasizes portability and versatility:

- **Camera Body**: Sony Alpha a6400 mirrorless
- **Primary Lenses**: 
  - Sony 16-50mm f/3.5-5.6 (everyday lens)
  - Sigma 30mm f/1.4 (low light and portraits)
  - Sony 18-105mm f/4 (versatile zoom)
- **Macro Equipment**: 
  - Extension tubes for close-up electronics photography
- **Other Gear**:
  - Tripod for long exposures and night photography
  - Portable LED lights for project documentation

## Learning Resources

For those interested in technical photography, especially for documenting engineering projects, I recommend:

- **DIY Photography** (diyphotography.net) - Great for lighting setups on a budget
- **Macro Photography for Electronics** by Dave Jones (YouTube)
- **Digital Photography School** (digital-photography-school.com) - Solid fundamentals

I believe photography is a universal language that can bridge the gap between technical and non-technical worlds. If you share this interest, I'd love to connect and discuss photography techniques or exchange ideas about documenting engineering projects visually.
